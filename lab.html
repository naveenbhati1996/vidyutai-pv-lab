Interactive Solar PV Lab

Smart Power Electronics Laboratory (SPEL), IIT Gandhinagar

About the lab

A 10 kW rooftop bifacial PV plant you can run: watch current flow from the modules to the grid, then add shading, faults and hot spots and see what they do to the I–V and P–V curves, the temperatures and the maximum power point tracker.

  • Curves overlap the STC reference at 1000 W/m², 25 °C cell
  • Shading creates a local peak; MPPT finds the global one
  • Sun position and shadows for Gandhinagar, hour by hour

Plant ratings

  • Plant: 18 × 565 W bifacial TOPCon, 3 strings × 6 → 10.2 kWp
  • Module: 144 half-cells, 3 bypass diodes
  • Voc / Isc: 50 V / 14 A at STC
  • Vmp / Imp: 42 V / 13.2 A at STC
  • Light load: 45–46 V measured

    What is happening

    Single-module test bench

    One 565 W module drives the load. A Tektronix probe reads current into logger CH9 and an isolated voltage sensor reads voltage into CH10.

    Load

    Logger scaling from the lab calibration: I = 126.67 × V(CH9), V = 31 × V(CH10).

    What the bench shows

    Load rating for this module

    The 45–46 V you read on the module is its voltage at light load, close to open circuit. At full power it works near 42 V and 13.2 A (about 555–565 W), so the load is sized from Voc, Isc and Pmax with margin.

    Ageing and lifetime

    Power fades slowly with sunlight and heat, and each module also has a chance of failing outright.

    Standards behind this lab
    
      
    What each standard covers and where this lab uses it. The lab is a teaching model; for design and test work use the full standard texts and the module datasheet.

    IEC 61215-1/-2:2021Design qualification of PV modules. MQT 09 hot-spot endurance: cells are shaded one at a time to find the worst case, then the module is exposed to 1000 ± 100 W/m² for 1 h (extended up to 5 h if cell temperature is still rising) at a module temperature of 55 ± 15 °C. Pass: no melted solder, delamination or burn marks; insulation and wet leakage still pass.Hot spot tab: worst-case shading curve and the test exposure.
    
            
    IEC 61215-2 MQT 11, 13Thermal cycling (−40 to +85 °C) and damp heat (85 °C, 85 % RH).85 °C line on the hot-spot chart: the top of the qualification temperature range.
    
            
    IEC 61730-1/-2Module safety qualification.Safety context for hot spots and failed bypass diodes.
    
            
    IEC TS 62446-3:2017Outdoor infrared thermography of PV modules and plants: stable, high irradiance (≥ 600 W/m² is the usual requirement), thermal steady state, soiling below 10 % Impp loss, and classification of findings into classes of abnormality (CoA 1–3).Thermal camera views and the hot-spot flag.
    
            
    IEC 62446-1Commissioning and periodic tests: polarity, string Voc and Isc, insulation resistance, optional I–V curve tracing.Faults tab: string Voc, Isc and current checks used for diagnosis.

    Assumptions in the model
    
      
      
        
    • Module values are the ones used in the lab: Voc 50 V, Isc 14 A, Vmp 42 V, Imp 13.2 A (Vmp × Imp = 554 W against the 565 W nameplate). 144 half-cells, two halves in parallel, three bypass diodes.
      • 
        
    • Coefficients are typical for n-type TOPCon, not from the datasheet: Voc −0.25 %/°C, Isc +0.046 %/°C, NMOT 43 °C, bifaciality 0.80. Replace them with the datasheet values when available.
      • 
        
    • Rear irradiance = albedo × front irradiance × 0.4 (view factor for a raised rooftop table).
      • 
        
    • Curves use a fast explicit approximation of the one-diode model. Its power temperature coefficient comes out near −0.2 %/°C, a little milder than a real TOPCon module (about −0.3 %/°C).
      • 
        
    • Hot spot: the shaded half-cell is a photocurrent source with a shunt resistance; its reverse voltage is limited by the other 23 cells of the substring plus a 0.45 V bypass diode, or by a 20 V breakdown when the diode is open. Thermal resistance 1.2 K/W in still air and a 6-minute time constant. Results are indicative, not a qualification test.
      • 
        
    • Inverter: one MPPT input for all three strings, 97.5 % conversion efficiency.
      • 
        
    • Drawings of the plant, modules and equipment are illustrations made by this page; they are not photographs.
